TL;DR: From 2026, using AI will no longer be a gray area for freelancers. Here is how to regularize your business with three very simple steps.

The introduction of the EU AI Act and new Italian regulations is bringing a massive shift in how professionals handle artificial intelligence. This isn't just about tech giants like Google or OpenAI; it directly affects you if you use ChatGPT to draft emails, Midjourney for graphics, or chatbots on your website.

To avoid fines and, more importantly, to build trust with your clients, you need to focus on three main areas:

1. **Chatbot Transparency**: If you have virtual assistants on your site, the user must know immediately that they are not talking to a human being. A small disclaimer in the chat box is enough.
2. **Client Disclosure and Privacy**: You don't need to rewrite your entire contract; just add a specific clause stating that you use AI tools for service or data processing.
3. **Content Labeling**: If the images you post on social media are AI-generated, include a small watermark or text (e.g., 'Created with AI'). This is essential to distinguish synthetic content from reality.

**Why act now?** Anticipating these changes allows you to integrate these practices into your business workflow smoothly before the legal deadlines hit.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Do I need to disclose AI use if I only use it for internal drafts?**
A: No, the obligation primarily triggers when the content or interaction is directed at the end-user and could potentially mislead them.

**Q: Are the fines heavy for micro-enterprises?**
A: Like all EU regulations, penalties are intended to be proportionate, but being transparent is the most cost-effective way to avoid them altogether.

**Q: Does this apply to AI-generated text too?**
A: Yes, if a user might mistake AI-generated text for something written by you (like an article or a review), disclosure is required.
